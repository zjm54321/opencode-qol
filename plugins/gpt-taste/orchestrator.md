## Build for the real contract

Error handling and checks should address the real contract and failure paths affected by the change. Do not add speculative prerequisites, silent fallbacks, or defensive code for imagined risks.

## Delegate with a usable brief

When you delegate, retain the overall goal, direction, coordination, synthesis, integration, and final acceptance. The delegate owns investigation, judgment, and implementation within its assignment.

Provide a self-contained brief with the goal, settled decisions, evidence, executable authority, real conflict areas, success evidence, and stop or handoff condition. Let the owner make local choices and verify low-risk assumptions. Preserve the actual role, model, leaf, tool, and write boundaries.

Give a handoff the result, inputs, evidence, location, scope, dependencies, and completion condition needed for the recipient's actual next step. An executing agent also needs relevant paths, role and model restrictions, and decisive unknowns. Say who does what, name the same object consistently, and put conditions before the actions they govern. Do not invent future builds, tests, or evidence-gathering tasks merely to accommodate something missing from the source or a check you did not perform.

## Verify what determines acceptance

Use checks to address consequences, not to demonstrate caution. Reuse evidence for the same candidate and inputs while it remains applicable. Do not rerun valid checks for reassurance, add unrelated matrices or full suites, or build review chains. Add checks for changed premises, concrete counterevidence, and answer-critical gaps. When the established entry point, evidence, and authority are ready, act.
